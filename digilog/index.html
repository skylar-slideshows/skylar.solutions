Instant-recall music mixing console with digital control of fully analog signal paths

Digilog, my team’s capstone project, fall 2026

So here is what I did

I did a large amount of the project, specifically nearly all the hardware. I just spent this entire Fall living and breathing digital system integration, MCUs, and mixed signal PCB design. I literally put entire weeks into KiCAD. I designed, from schematic to physical assembly and validation, a total of ten individual PCBs (not counting the ones with < 4 layers) in the past four months, all for this project. These include:

  • Two different STM32 boards with displays (FMC/parallel), I2C, SPI, USB-C, and UART;
  • Five low noise, low crosstalk, highly linear, analog audio processing “mini-boards” and two larger analog routing motherboards; and
  • A user controls board utilizing MCP23017 GPIO expanders, rotary encoders, and shift-register driven LED displays.

In addition to this, I wrote a large amount of the firmware, including designing the I2C polling scheme, a few FreeRTOS tasks, and writing low level hardware code. I also wrote code that allows the console to receive timecode and transport commands from industry standard Digital Audio Workstation (DAW) programs, and I wrote a standalone console remote control and configuration computer program.

Through all of this, I had to break what seemed like a monumental monolith of a goal into little pieces for both myself and my teammates. I had to then coordinate all of these little pieces to come back together into the final product. The school’s capstone goal is to make a functional prototype, but we ended up making somewhat of a highly featured, definitely not cost optimized, functional proto-product.

To be able to do all of this in a single semester, I used Claude initially as an idea “bouncing” board to help me build the first ideas. (Note however that this concept is my own original idea). Later in the semester, I came up with creative ways to use AI to increase productivity through the automation of annoying tasks. For example, a recording console has a lot of parallelism in its channels, so there were a lot of places I routed one channel or drew one schematic and told Claude to copy the routing over to the other identical channels under their corresponding nets.

I am confident and have decent experience with using AI tools (GitHub Copilot and Claude Code) to help me write code. I know how to get these tools to do the right things and go the right direction. I can make .md skill files to explain more complex criteria to AI or give necessary technical context to my teammates’ AIs to also work on the project. At this point, I can “PCBify” nearly anything, and I feel confident in being able to at least help undertake designing complex systems like this, but of course, I have a lot I can also learn from more senior engineers.
